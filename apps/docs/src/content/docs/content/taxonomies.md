---
title: Tags and categories
description: Classifying content with terms, and what tagging does and does not do.
---

A **taxonomy** is a vocabulary — Departments, Topics, Audiences. A **term** is one word in it —
Sciences, Admissions, Prospective Students.

Your site's taxonomies were set up by an administrator. Some are flat lists; some are trees, where
Biology sits under Sciences sits under Academics.

## Tagging content

Through a tags field on the item. Some accept one term, some several. Tree taxonomies show the
hierarchy so you can pick the level you mean.

## Filtering by a term

Content lists filter by term. **A term filter always includes the whole branch beneath it** — filter
by Sciences and you get everything filed under Biology and Chemistry too.

That is almost always what you want. Somebody filing a page under Biology expects it to turn up when
a colleague looks for Sciences.

## Managing terms

**Library → Taxonomies**, then the taxonomy. You can add terms, rename them, reorder them, and nest
them if the taxonomy is hierarchical.

Renaming a term is safe — everything tagged with it follows, because content references the term
rather than its name.

Deleting a term removes it from everything tagged with it. Taproot tells you how many items that is
first.

## What tagging does not do

**It does not control who can edit.** Tagging a page "Admissions" does not restrict it to the
admissions team, and there is no setting that would make it. Roles in Taproot are site-wide; see
[What your role can do](/start/roles/).

This is worth being explicit about because it is a natural assumption. If tags granted permission,
then any contributor — who can change any tag — could hand themselves or anyone else access by
retagging a page. Classification and permission are kept apart on purpose.

## Whether terms have their own pages

Depends on your site. Some sites give every term in a taxonomy an archive page listing what is filed
under it; some give no taxonomy one.

Taproot has no opinion here — most taxonomies (review status, internal owner, audience segment)
classify content usefully without deserving a page each. Whoever built your site decided which, if
any, get one.
